六、口径与已知缺口

  • Blast heights of burst are scaled as W^(1/3), so the 1 Mt rows sit at 10,200 ft (5 psi) and 15,750 ft (1 psi). §3.73 (p.110) states the homogeneous sea-level atmosphere assumption for burst heights of 5,000 ft or less, so the 1 Mt blast rows extrapolate the handbook's assumption by a factor of 2-3. No altitude correction (§3.64-3.66) is applied; the sign of that omission is that the 1 Mt radii are understated rather than overstated.
  • 5 psi is not printed on Fig. 3.73c. It is interpolated between the 4 and 6 psi contours at the log-pressure position 0.5503, and every 5 psi row is labelled as interpolated.
  • Thermal radii are slant ranges. §12.66 (p.563) notes that for ranges much greater than the burst height they may be taken as distances to ground zero, which is how they are reported here.
  • Thermal radii are for air bursts only. Fig. 7.42 is calculated for burst heights of 200·W^0.4 ft and the handbook's surface-burst treatment of thermal radiation (§7.41, p.290) points to a different procedure, so the ground-burst option shows blast and fireball only.
  • Thermal contour values on Fig. 7.42 are not printed as legible labels on the scan; they were fixed by two independent statements in the handbook (§12.66 p.563: 4.5-6 cal/cm² at 9-10 mi for 1 Mt; §7.43 p.290: 8 cal/cm² at about 7 mi). Both are reproduced within ±5% by the digitized curves.
